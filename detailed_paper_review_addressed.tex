\section*{Scope and overall assessment}
The manuscript has a promising objective: risk bounds for conditional-quantile estimation with temporal dependence, a shared spatial random effect, heavy-tailed measurement error, and several structured function classes. The current compiled version, however, contains foundational mathematical inconsistencies in the dependence assumptions, coupling, localization, and application-specific calculations. It also contains literal internal review notes in the compiled appendix. These issues are more serious than ordinary copyediting.

The companion full-length redline preserves the paper's section order---general theory, trend filtering, high-dimensional linear quantile regression, $K$-NN fused lasso, nonconvex classes, neural networks, algorithms, experiments, and the complete proof supplement---in one TeX file. In the main paper, mathematically invalid or internally inconsistent statements are replaced by corrected model, assumption, theorem, and algorithm blocks. Appendices A--I now contain operative corrected statements and proofs in red; the previous appendix comments have been replaced by actual derivations. A final gray tracked-comparison appendix retains the supplied detailed derivations solely so that every change can be located against the original source. The experimental numerical values and designs are not altered; experiment-specific concerns are listed separately below.


\begin{center}

\textbf{Most important substantive changes in the revised paper:}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item beta mixing is imposed on the \emph{joint time cluster} $W_i=(X_i,\delta_i,E_i)$, Berbee coupling is applied to that joint object, and the design law used after conditioning is made explicit;
\item the empirical processes are centered at $f^*$ and localized through an explicit critical radius;
\item the tail remainder is quantitatively tied to the curvature margin;
\item prediction and parameter-recovery claims are separated in the high-dimensional linear section;
\item graph-TV scaling and normalized loss conventions are corrected;
\item the nonconvex result uses a genuine near-best approximating function and a centered star hull;
\item the neural-network class has a specified, clipped, sparse architecture; and
\item the ADMM dual step is corrected.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Scope of the mathematical repair.} The internal logical gaps identified in the appendix have been addressed: the proof now uses joint-cluster coupling, centered weighted processes, consistent conditioning, explicit graph and network conditions, and a fixed-point radius. Source-dependent ingredients are isolated as named assumptions rather than asserted without support. The resulting document is a mathematically coherent revision, but---as with any new theoretical argument---the authors should still check cited external approximation, entropy, and graph-geometry inputs against the primary sources before submission.
